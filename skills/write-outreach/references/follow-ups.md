# Follow-ups

## The 3-D test

Every follow-up must pass all three.

- **Different.** New angle, new information, new proof. Never the same mail twice. "Just following up" is burned sending capacity.
- **Disarming.** Light, a little self-deprecating, no pressure.
- **Direct.** No filler, no needy language. Grammar may break, meaning may not.

Two more rules:
- Every step stands alone. A reader who deleted step 1 must still understand step 2.
- A follow-up never restates the features or the pain from step 1. Follow-ups that repeat features have produced zero opportunities in every set we have measured. Shift the angle.

## How many

| Audience | Steps | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Direct SMB, email | 2 (step 2 rarely adds opportunities, step 3 adds none) | 3 days |
| Mid-market, email-led | 3, ceiling 4 | 3-4 days |
| Small TAM, partners, associations | longer is acceptable | 4-7 days |
| LinkedIn-first | up to 7 touches across channels | 0, 2, 3, 7, 27 days |

If the first three emails across 7-10 days did not land, the next five will not either.

## Nine follow-up angles

Pick one per follow-up. Note which you used in the copy file so the next campaign does not repeat it.

1. **One-line question.** "Is this a problem at {{companyName}} or have I got the wrong end of it?"
2. **Deliver the lead magnet.** "Here is the write-up I mentioned." Only if you actually built it.
3. **Industry insight.** One sourced observation about their trade this month.
4. **Value-prop extension.** "By the way, we also handle X." Only if X is a second real angle, not a feature.
5. **Refer to a colleague plus three ideas.** "If this sits with someone else, here are three things they would want to know."
6. **Competitive intel.** "Two of your peers in {{city}} switched this quarter." Only with real, nameable peers.
7. **Customer quote.** One verbatim sentence from a customer in their trade.
8. **Previous employer or previous tool.** "You used X at your last place; this is the version for a shop your size."
9. **Number nudge.** "Is {{phone}} still the right number to reach you?" Works when you hold the number from a public source and it opens a phone follow-up.

For direct SMB the reliable ones are 1, 3, 7, and 9.

## Reminder-style retargets

Reminder retargets ("did you see my last mail", "bumping this") produce replies and no opportunities. They are the purest reply-vanity trap. Do not run them as a campaign. If you re-approach a pool after the 60-day window, write a new angle, not a reminder.

## Breakup emails

Run them if you like, but measure meetings, not replies. They produce "not now" replies that inflate reply rate and nothing else. Never the primary CTA of a sequence.

## Examples

Fictional. Step 1 asked whether after-hours calls go unanswered at a bike shop.

Step 2, angle 1 (one-line question), day 3, same thread, empty subject:

> Wrong person, or wrong problem? If someone else looks after the phones at Juniper Bikes, a name is enough.

Step 2, angle 7 (customer quote):

> A shop owner in Leeds told us last month: "Saturday afternoons we lost two repair bookings a week to voicemail." If that sounds familiar, reply with "yes" and I will send how she fixed it.

Step 2, angle 9 (number nudge):

> Is 0113 000 0000 still the right number for the workshop? I would rather call than keep writing.
