# Anti-slop eval

Run this after writing or editing any cold email, follow-up, LinkedIn invite note or LinkedIn message. Answer each line pass or fail. Any fail: fix the draft and run the list again. Return the draft only when every line passes. For a detect request, return the named patterns with quoted lines and short fixes instead, and do not rewrite.

## Voice and meaning

1. The draft states the writer's point without added claims, examples, statistics, quotes or opinions.
2. The writer's vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humour and hedges survive the edit.
3. Strong human sentences were left alone rather than tidied for consistency.
4. The amount of cutting matches the amount of slop; character was not compressed away.
5. The message leads with the observation about the recipient, not with the sender.
6. Every specific fact and number in the source draft is still there and still exact.
7. Sentences use human subjects and active verbs where the draft allows it.

## Words

8. No banned word from `anti-slop-writing.md` appears, unless quoted as an example.
9. Every "just", "really", "actually", "honestly", "truly" was deleted and the sentence reread.
10. No filler phrase delays the point ("I wanted to reach out", "it's worth noting", "in order to").
11. No opener or sign-off cliché ("hope this finds you well", "quick question", "circling back", "excited to connect").

## Patterns

12. No binary contrast, negative listing, rhetorical setup or throat-clearing opener.
13. No faux-insight setup, colon reveal, trailing "-ing" analysis, fake-strong verb, synonym cycling, dramatic fragment or robotic rhythm.
14. No importance puffery. No weasel attribution: every claim names its source or was cut.
15. No fake-profound kicker. The message ends on the ask or on the most concrete sentence.
16. No summary recap. No emoji, no decorative bold, no bullets in a message under 90 words.
17. Dash count is zero, including substitute punctuation doing the kicker job.
18. Colons introduce lists or quotes only, sentence case after them.

## Cold outreach

19. Line 1 could not be true for 10,000 other recipients, and it is verified for this one.
20. One sentence says what the product is.
21. One ask, answerable in one word, with the opt-out costing one word.
22. 40-70 words per email, under 40 for a LinkedIn message, no link in touch 1.
23. Each follow-up brings a different angle and stands alone.

## Final read

24. Read aloud, it sounds like one capable person writing to another.
25. No three sentences in a row share the same shape.
26. The writer would recognise the draft as their own.
27. The output includes the full draft plus a three-line "What changed" (edit mode), or named patterns with quoted lines and fixes (detect mode).
